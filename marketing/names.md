# Names

*2026-09-24 · Candidates for the product's name, from the "a warehouse for one" positioning. Lakelet stays the working name until a box is ticked; the deadline is the ship brief's S7 (PyPI, brew, the signing identity, the domain). Registry checks were made on this date from this machine: PyPI via its JSON API, `.com` at Verisign's RDAP, `.dev` at Google Registry's RDAP, `.io` via rdap.org. A "taken" PyPI name may be a squat; `<name>-cli` is the fallback TASKS.md already names. No trademark search has been made; that is the open item in TASKS.md and is manual (USPTO, classes 9 and 42).*

## The test a name must pass

1. It names the judgment, not the storage. "Lakelet" names the storage half, the part a tutorial replaces.
2. It survives the roadmap: still true when the tables are in a bucket, a worker runs the Red query, and the one asking is an agent.
3. It is free, or cheaply free, on PyPI, brew, GitHub, one good domain, and USPTO classes 9 and 42.
4. It works as a command: `<name> init`, `<name> sql "…"`, `<name> run`.
5. It does not compound with feature names. The gauge stays "the verdict" or "Lookahead".

## The families

- **The measurement before you go** (the verdict): sounding, plumb, sextant, leadline, sounder, lookahead.
- **The load and the hull** (does it fit): loadline, plimsoll, freeboard, draftmark, waterline, headroom, loadmark.
- **A vessel for one** (a warehouse for one): skiff, coracle, dinghy, tarn, wharf, larder.
- **Coined**: prefit.

## Ranked

1. **Sounding.** Names the verdict exactly: a sounding is the depth you take before you proceed, and "sound" also means solid. Keeps the water without the lake. PyPI free, sounding.io free. The costs: a common word the trademark office will weigh, an audio association, and a gerund that some will find soft. As a command it reads well: `sounding sql`, `sounding run`. The gauge's name becomes "a sounding".
2. **Tarn.** Four letters, ownable, nothing found but a theme. A tarn is a small lake high up on its own, which is "a warehouse for one" in a word, and almost nobody knows the word, so the "laptop lakehouse" commodity does not attach to it. The costs: it is still water and still a lake, and it has no verb.
3. **Plumb.** Verb-able and exact: to plumb is to measure depth, and plumb means true. `plumb sql "…"` reads as a sentence. The costs: PyPI taken, a possible AI product of the same name, and the plumbing jokes will never stop.
4. **Coracle.** A boat for one, distinctive, no big company. The cost: a live open-source Nostr client of the same name.
5. **Prefit.** Clean on every registry and says "fits, before". The cost: it means little and sounds like golf clubs.
6. **Skiff.** The best word for "a warehouse for one" and the worst situation: a well-known privacy brand shut down by Notion, which likely still holds the mark.

Not recommended, and why: Plimsoll (a business-analytics firm owns the association), Freeboard and Waterline and Headroom and Keel (live projects with the exact name), Draftmark (a live product in the agent space), Larder (live), Loadline and Loadmark (the wrong industry), Wharf (too many small collisions and an adware).

### Ranked, round two

1. **Warelet.** "Ware" from warehouse, "-let" for small: a little warehouse, which is "a warehouse for one" in one word, and the rename explains itself to anyone who knew Lakelet: not a small lake, a small warehouse. Clean everywhere checked, zero repositories, nothing on the web. Costs: "-let" reads small or toy to some, which Lakelet already accepted; "ware" can be heard as software.
2. **Boathouse.** The strongest plain-English "-house" cue: everyone knows what one is, it is small, it is by the water, and it keeps one boat. Clean on PyPI, .io and .dev. Costs: nine letters, and the .com belongs to a clothing retailer.
3. **Springhouse.** The best story, a household's own cold store over its own source, and a "-house". Costs: a consumer AI app of the same name launching in 2026 and a consulting firm that sells data training under it, both live.
4. **Prequery.** The verdict as a name, with "query" as the cue. Cost: it names one feature, not the warehouse or the record.
5. **Minimart.** A data mart for one, in a word everyone knows. Costs: generic in retail software, a crowded class 9, and it sounds cheap.

Ruled out: Icehouse (Starburst), Datashed (ambiguous), the Supabase-shaped pair.
